# Demo chiller maintenance guide

> Synthetic demonstration document written for this dashboard's demo mode. It is not a manufacturer manual, a standard or site documentation. On a real plant, follow the equipment manufacturer's instructions.

## Performance indicators

Chiller efficiency is tracked as the coefficient of performance (COP): the cooling delivered divided by the electrical power drawn. kW per ton expresses the same quantity the other way round, so a rising kW/ton means falling efficiency.

The condenser approach is the difference between the saturated condensing temperature and the leaving condenser water temperature. The evaporator approach is the difference between the leaving chilled water temperature and the saturated evaporating temperature. Both are logged once per shift and compared with the commissioning values.

## Common causes of low COP

Condenser tube fouling insulates the tubes, which raises the condenser approach and the head pressure, so the compressor needs more power for the same load and low COP results.

Non-condensable gases collect in the condenser and raise the condensing pressure; a high condenser approach with a clean tube bundle indicates them.

A low refrigerant charge lowers the evaporating pressure and causes a high evaporator approach and reduced efficiency.

High condenser water temperature from the cooling towers raises the refrigerant lift, so each ton of cooling costs more compressor power.

A low chilled-water delta-T at part load makes extra chillers run lightly loaded, which lowers the efficiency of the whole plant.

## Condenser tube cleaning procedure

This procedure removes condenser tube fouling and scale deposits. Clean the tubes when the condenser approach has risen well above its commissioning value and non-condensable gases have been ruled out.

1. Stage the chiller off and confirm that another chiller has taken the load.
2. Stop the condenser water pump that feeds this chiller and close the condenser isolation valves.
3. Drain the water side of the condenser and open the water boxes.
4. Brush the tubes, flush them with clean water and inspect the tube sheet for scale deposits.
5. Close the water boxes, refill and vent the condenser, and return the chiller to service.
6. Record the condenser approach at a comparable load and compare it with the value before cleaning.

## Refrigerant leak inspection

A refrigerant leak shows as a low refrigerant charge: low suction pressure, a high evaporator approach and, over time, reduced efficiency. Check flanged joints, valve packings and the run time of the purge unit, which rises when air enters a low-pressure machine.

Refrigerant handling follows the site's refrigerant safety rules and the applicable standard, for example EN 378 in Europe.

## Compressor surge

Compressor surge in a centrifugal chiller is a reversal of refrigerant flow at high lift and low load. It is heard as a loud noise and felt as vibration, and repeated surging damages the compressor. High condenser water temperature raises the lift and makes surge more likely. Condenser tube fouling has the same effect.
